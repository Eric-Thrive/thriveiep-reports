Well-informed observers: the right perspective for the right behavior

A role-aware evidence review for adding adult informant perspectives to C2A without mistaking context, workplace performance, or private experience for the same thing.

Executive answer

Do not ask, “Who is the observer?” Ask, “Who has seen this behavior, in this setting, often enough to describe it?”

The research supports well-informed observers as valuable, context-specific perspectives. It does not support a generic observer score. A partner may know everyday organization. A manager may know workplace planning and follow-through. A coworker may know collaboration. Those are different windows, not interchangeable measurements.

Core rule: retain rater role, setting, and opportunity-to-observe data separately at first. Combine perspectives only after a study establishes that they mean the same thing for the intended use.

What advances

Benchmarking

Licensed forms can help test which domains, anchors, rater roles, and outcomes deserve a local validation study.

What does not advance

Automatic transfer

Do not import a family-informant norm, a manager performance rating, or a self-report factor model as a C2A observer score.

Questions 2–6 · Measurement design

Good observer data begins with exposure, not a score

1 · Capture metadata

Record the context

Role, relationship duration, contact frequency, settings observed, domain-specific opportunity, confidence/basis, and incentives or conflicts.

2 · Describe behavior

Use bounded observation

Behavior-specific, fixed-window anchors are defensible. “No opportunity to observe” is structural missingness, not a low rating.

3 · Do not overread

Keep roles separate

Do not pool family, peer, and manager reports by default. Their disagreement may describe setting, standards, or opportunity.

Evidence trail

Key sources and boundaries

The full research rerun used direct academic and official sources. The evidence is strongest for adult informant EF and workplace multisource performance ratings; it is weakest for role-specific postsecondary C2A use.
