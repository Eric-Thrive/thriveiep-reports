Agentic Coding

Cheap Code, Costly Judgment: A Case Study on Governable Agentic Software Engineering
ARXIV2026-07-01

James C. Davis, Paschal C. Amusuo et al. · arXiv preprint

A 12-week case study in which one expert engineer used frontier coding agents to build a document accessibility system, producing 420 KLOC of code plus 1.16 MLOC of tests and supporting material. The authors develop a governance conversion model: recurring structural failure classes emerge under high-velocity agentic implementation, and engineers stay afloat by converting each failure class into a durable governance mechanism. Generation gets cheap; review and governance become the binding cost.

The Accommodation Engine took 1,646 non-merge commits in this same 28-day window. Governance conversion is the shape our own repo rules, gates and skill-learnings files already have.

Why Does CLAUDE.md Keep Growing? Catastrophic Remembering in Agentic Coding
ARXIV2026-08-01

Chakrabarti · arXiv preprint

Traced 247,694 instruction lifetimes across 1,867 repositories and found agentic-coding instruction files grow without bound: +226% over their lifetime and +4.9 net instructions per commit. The mechanism named is catastrophic remembering, where deleting an instruction risks a correctness regression once its rationale is forgotten, so nothing is ever removed. Attaching rationale comments to instructions removed 99.3% of excess growth in a controlled inversion and improved real-world instruction-following by up to 23.1%.

This repo carries a root CLAUDE.md plus six global rules files and several .claude/rules entries. The rationale-comment fix is directly testable here.

The 2026-07-28 MCP Specification
BLOG2026-07-28

Model Context Protocol maintainers

The fifth MCP spec drops the initialize/initialized handshake and the Mcp-Session-Id header, moving the core to plain request/response so servers can run serverless and behind ordinary load balancers. Roots, Sampling and Logging leave core, legacy HTTP+SSE gets a 12-month offramp, Dynamic Client Registration is deprecated in favor of Client ID Metadata Documents, and Tasks, Apps and Enterprise-Managed Authorization become versioned extensions. New Mcp-Method and Mcp-Name routing headers let gateways meter calls without parsing JSON bodies, and list/read responses gain ttlMs and cacheScope. TypeScript, Python, Go and C# SDKs shipped support on release day; Rust is beta.

Every MCP server this workspace depends on is affected. Dynamic Client Registration deprecation and the loss of Mcp-Session-Id are the two that touch authenticated servers first.

Document Processing and OCR

Can You Trust the Confidence? ConfBench for Vision-Language Models on Document Extraction
ARXIV2026-08-01

Roy, Martin et al. · arXiv preprint

The first calibration-specific benchmark for VLM key-information extraction, built from 20 controlled degradation pipelines producing 1,346 document variants and over 70,000 entity-level evaluations. Feeding OCR text alongside the image yields better-calibrated confidence than image-only input, and within the Claude family confidence quality scales monotonically with capability, while raw parameter count predicts poorly across families.

The redactor pipeline decides what to auto-accept versus route to human review. Calibrated confidence, and the finding that OCR text plus image beats image-only, bears directly on that threshold.

Does noncredible reporting moderate the relationship of executive functioning ratings to executive functioning tests, psychological symptoms, or functional impairment in adults seeking assessment for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder?
JOURNAL2026-08-01

Julie A. Suhr, Adrienne Jankowski et al. · Psychological Assessment 38(8), 562-570

In an archival sample of 99 adults evaluated for ADHD, executive-function rating-scale scores were largely unrelated to objective EF test scores regardless of symptom-validity group. Emotional distress or executive dysfunction? What is the Behavior Rating Inventory of Executive Function-Adult self-report scale actually assessing in an assessment-seeking postsecondary sample?
JOURNAL2026-08-01

Allyson G. Harrison, Irene Armstrong et al. · Psychological Assessment 38(8), 571-582

Across 826 assessment-seeking postsecondary students with archival BRIEF-A, Personality Assessment Inventory and objective EF test data, negative response bias significantly elevated every BRIEF-A scale and index. Once NRB was controlled, all BRIEF-A indices were unrelated to objective EF performance and were instead predicted by general psychological distress. The authors conclude that in a postsecondary population the BRIEF-A reflects psychological symptoms and response bias, not measured executive function.

826 assessment-seeking postsecondary students, the exact C2A population. Read alongside the Suhr paper in the same issue before the next scoring-layer review.

JOURNAL2026-08-12

Mohammed Salah, Rudaina Al-Busaidi et al. · Frontiers in Education (perspective article)

Argues that AI systems meant to support self-regulation can instead perform the regulatory work students need to develop themselves.
